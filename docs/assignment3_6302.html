Methods note

The goal of this was to examine rural urban splits at the county level. Geography: County (State: California) Variables:

  • Renter occupied housing units

  • Number of households

  • Number of enrolled students

A rent rate is calculated as the percentage of households renting. (renter occupied) / (total households) Assumptions: Large percent of renters and a larger number of students in urban counties, showing a younger and more transient population. The margin of error show the uncertainty around the values being mapped. This and the related uncertainty intervals can show if counties are statically different in their rent rates (or not significant if there is an overlap) Interpretation: The map demonstrates the number of students is higher in those urban counties, this is more a reflection of the population density but can also be viewed as the population is younger. Most of the top 10 in rent percent also are major urban counties. (Namely, San Francisco County & Los Angeles)

upper <- list()
lower <- list()

for (n in 1:length(acs_wide$rent_moe)) {
  moe <- acs_wide$rent_moe[n]
  pe <- acs_wide$rent_rate[n]
  lower[[length(lower) + 1]] <- pe - moe
  upper[[length(upper) + 1]] <- pe + moe
}

acs_wide$rent_upper <- upper
acs_wide$rent_lower <- lower
